# 03 · Cinematography and camera movement

Design the viewer's access to a beat. Set camera position, orientation, frame, focus and movement with the action and edit in mind. Shot size describes what appears in the frame. Camera position and lens choice are separate controls.

Russian retrieval terms include общий план (wide shot), средний план (medium shot), крупный план (close-up), направление движения в кадре (screen direction), ось действия (action axis), панорамирование (pan), перевод фокуса (focus pull), and проезд камеры (tracking or dolly movement, depending on path).

## Rules

### CIN-CAM-001

**Choose shot size by the information needed** · established technique · priority 1 · high confidence

Set the frame scale around the action, relationship or detail that must read.

**Why it works.** A wide frame can show geography and body action. A close frame makes a small cue available and can feel intimate or intrusive in context.

**Apply.** Shot-list planning. **Exception.** Sustained distance can make the audience observe or infer. Check the necessary detail remains readable.

**Example.** A wide keeps a door and performer together until a close shot makes a hesitation visible. **Common mistake.** Choosing a close-up for an abstract emotion without checking what changes within the frame.

**LLM action.** Specify visible body boundaries and essential objects at start and end. Treat size as a composition target. **Check.** At final display scale the audience can see all cues that justify the shot.

**Origin.** ACMI shot-size exercises and AFI shot rationale. [SRC-ACMI-SHOT](../sources/bibliography.md#src-acmi-shot) · [SRC-AFI-DIR](../sources/bibliography.md#src-afi-dir)

**Related.** [CIN-CAM-002](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-002), [CIN-CAM-003](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-003), [CIN-CAM-008](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-008), [CIN-CAM-018](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-018), [DIR-010](02_directing.md#dir-010)

### CIN-CAM-002

**Use angle and height to define relations** · established technique · priority 2 · high confidence

Choose height, yaw, pitch and any roll by the relationship the viewer should perceive.

**Why it works.** Height and angle change visibility, proportions and alignment. A low angle can increase apparent scale or align us with someone seated.

**Apply.** Power shifts, subjective alignment and geographic clarity. **Exception.** A deliberately neutral or fixed vantage can let behavior carry the shift.

**Example.** A child's eye-level view hides what an adult sees over a countertop. **Common mistake.** Using a low angle as a universal instruction for power.

**LLM action.** Describe camera height relative to the eyes, direction and visible surfaces. Add roll only when planned. **Check.** Check viewpoint, horizon and surfaces against the floor plan and dramatic purpose.

**Origin.** ASC camera placement examples. [SRC-ASC-POS](../sources/bibliography.md#src-asc-pos) · [SRC-ACMI-SHOT](../sources/bibliography.md#src-acmi-shot)

**Related.** [CIN-CAM-001](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-001), [CIN-CAM-005](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-005), [CIN-CAM-010](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-010), [DIR-002](02_directing.md#dir-002)

### CIN-CAM-003

**Establish geography when it matters** · established technique · priority 1 · high confidence

Show the relevant positions, routes and relationships before fragmenting them. Re-establish after a meaningful spatial change.

**Why it works.** A viewer can assemble tighter shots around anchors already seen.

**Apply.** New locations, group scenes, pursuit and changed blocking. **Exception.** Delay orientation for surprise or subjective confusion and plan the moment of clarification.

**Example.** Show both sides of the counter and the exit before cutting to hands below it. **Common mistake.** Beginning with attractive scenery that never connects to the action space.

**LLM action.** List the anchors a wide, sound cue or motivated movement must establish. Add a new geography beat when the axis changes. **Check.** A new viewer can sketch the locations that matter and track who can see or reach what.

**Origin.** ACMI establishing shot and Smith's account of analytical editing. [SRC-ACMI-SHOT](../sources/bibliography.md#src-acmi-shot) · [SRC-SMITH-THESIS](../sources/bibliography.md#src-smith-thesis)

**Related.** [CIN-CAM-004](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-004), [CIN-CAM-005](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-005), [CIN-CAM-009](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-009), [CIN-CAM-018](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-018)

### CIN-CAM-004

**Track the action axis and screen direction** · continuity convention · priority 1 · high confidence

For a continuous exchange or motion, choose the dominant axis and default camera side. Preserve apparent direction across cuts.

**Why it works.** Stable left-right relations help the viewer maintain orientation across independently framed views.

**Apply.** Dialogues, pursuit, entrances, exits and handoffs. **Exception.** Cross during a visible continuous move, after a clear re-establishment, or to create intentional disorientation. Check the result.

**Example.** Two characters keep opposing screenward eyelines until an on-screen move establishes the new side. **Common mistake.** Mirroring a reference image and reversing a prop hand or facing direction.

**LLM action.** Store axis anchors, camera side, screen entry and exit, and any axis-change method per shot. **Check.** Draw both camera positions against the axis and compare the adjacent rendered frames.

**Origin.** Classical axis convention described by Smith. [SRC-SMITH-THESIS](../sources/bibliography.md#src-smith-thesis) · [SRC-SMITH-AT](../sources/bibliography.md#src-smith-at)

**Related.** [CIN-CAM-003](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-003), [CIN-CAM-005](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-005), [CIN-CAM-006](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-006), [CIN-CAM-007](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-007), [CIN-CAM-014](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-014)

### CIN-CAM-005

**Match eyelines and reverse angles** · continuity convention · priority 1 · high confidence

Plan who looks at whom, gaze height and screenward direction across complementary angles. Use a shoulder when it clarifies the relation.

**Why it works.** The look can connect an off-screen subject to a reverse or POV without showing both together.

**Apply.** Dialogue, reactions, looks to objects and reveal setups. **Exception.** An unmatched look may signal distraction, a lie or deliberately misleading alignment. Give that choice evidence.

**Example.** A looks screen right toward taller B. B's reverse looks screen left and slightly down. **Common mistake.** Giving both characters the same gaze direction in a single exchange.

**LLM action.** Specify gaze target by ID, offset from lens, height, camera side and visible shoulder if needed. **Check.** Pause both shots and trace their lines of sight. They should meet the planned target in the shared map.

**Origin.** Smith on eyeline matching and shot/reverse-shot. [SRC-SMITH-THESIS](../sources/bibliography.md#src-smith-thesis) · [SRC-SMITH-AT](../sources/bibliography.md#src-smith-at)

**Related.** [CIN-CAM-002](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-002), [CIN-CAM-003](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-003), [CIN-CAM-004](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-004), [CIN-CAM-018](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-018), [DIR-002](02_directing.md#dir-002)

### CIN-CAM-006

**Avoid accidental near-duplicate cuts** · continuity convention · priority 2 · high confidence

For consecutive views of the same subject, change the angle or scale enough for the cut to read intentionally. About 30 degrees is a craft starting point for angular change.

**Why it works.** A distinct view reduces the chance of a distracting jump when nearly identical framing changes suddenly.

**Apply.** Analytical coverage and repair of near-matching shots. **Exception.** A jump can express time loss, fracture or comedy. Keep a purposeful difference the viewer can register.

**Example.** Cut from a three-quarter medium view to a distinctly tighter insert with new information. **Common mistake.** Changing the position slightly and treating 30 degrees as a pass/fail law.

**LLM action.** Compare adjacency for angle, framing, subject pose and perceptual attention. Choose a cutaway or reframe when it bumps. **Check.** Play the cut at speed. It should land as intended, whether smooth or deliberately abrupt.

**Origin.** The 30-degree rule described in Smith's thesis. [SRC-SMITH-THESIS](../sources/bibliography.md#src-smith-thesis) · [SRC-SMITH-AT](../sources/bibliography.md#src-smith-at)

**Related.** [CIN-CAM-004](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-004), [CIN-CAM-007](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-007), [CIN-CAM-018](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-018)

### CIN-CAM-007

**Match action across a cut** · continuity convention · priority 1 · high confidence

Cut on compatible phases of a continuous action. Preserve the actor, hand, target, direction and object state.

**Why it works.** The motion gives attention a thread through the change of viewpoint.

**Apply.** Handoffs, entrances, sitting, opening and physical contact. **Exception.** An ellipsis can omit part of the action when the new state and elapsed time are clear.

**Example.** Cut during a reach and resume in the new angle before contact. **Common mistake.** Showing a fully open door in the second frame while the first ends before the latch turns.

**LLM action.** Plan overlap handles and a shared action anchor. Describe both the pre-contact and post-contact state. **Check.** Inspect two frames around the cut and play at speed. Check for a duplicated, skipped or reversed action.

**Origin.** Smith's attentional account of match-action and exits/entrances. [SRC-SMITH-AT](../sources/bibliography.md#src-smith-at) · [SRC-SMITH-THESIS](../sources/bibliography.md#src-smith-thesis)

**Related.** [CIN-CAM-004](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-004), [CIN-CAM-005](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-005), [CIN-CAM-006](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-006), [CIN-CAM-013](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-013)

### CIN-CAM-008

**Compose a readable visual hierarchy** · established technique · priority 1 · high confidence

Use scale, tonal contrast, line, negative space, symmetry or asymmetry to prioritize what the viewer should notice.

**Why it works.** Relative contrast and placement can guide attention or leave a deliberate absence legible.

**Apply.** Boards, first frames and recrops. **Exception.** Balanced distribution can invite scanning. Centering can express order, confrontation or stillness when the beat calls for it.

**Example.** Leave open space in the direction of a look until a planned entrance fills it. **Common mistake.** Applying a thirds grid to every shot regardless of the intended focus.

**LLM action.** Specify the first target, secondary target, planned empty area and composition constraints in the delivery aspect ratio. **Check.** View the frame small. The intended emphasis survives crop, contrast and motion.

**Origin.** ACMI framing exercises and Block's contrast and affinity. [SRC-ACMI-COMP](../sources/bibliography.md#src-acmi-comp) · [SRC-BLOCK-VIS](../sources/bibliography.md#src-block-vis)

**Related.** [CIN-CAM-001](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-001), [CIN-CAM-002](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-002), [CIN-CAM-009](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-009), [CIN-CAM-011](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-011), [DIR-009](02_directing.md#dir-009)

### CIN-CAM-009

**Stage depth with readable layers** · established technique · priority 2 · high confidence

Use foreground, middle and background relationships, overlap, scale and motion to show depth or intentionally flatten it.

**Why it works.** Depth can clarify geography, isolate a figure or let concurrent actions play within one frame.

**Apply.** Staging reveals, ensemble shots and establishing geography. **Exception.** A flat frontal composition can emphasize pattern, tableau or alienation.

**Example.** A doorway frames a middle-ground exchange while a distant watcher remains readable. **Common mistake.** Listing three layers without deciding which action each supports.

**LLM action.** Give each important layer an object ID, distance relation, focus state and occlusion rule. **Check.** Compare start and end frames for preserved depth, intended occlusion and readable action.

**Origin.** Block's visual structure and ASC placement practice. [SRC-BLOCK-VIS](../sources/bibliography.md#src-block-vis) · [SRC-ASC-POS](../sources/bibliography.md#src-asc-pos) · [SRC-ACMI-COMP](../sources/bibliography.md#src-acmi-comp)

**Related.** [CIN-CAM-003](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-003), [CIN-CAM-008](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-008), [CIN-CAM-010](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-010), [CIN-CAM-011](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-011), [CIN-CAM-014](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-014)

### CIN-CAM-010

**Set perspective with camera position** · technical constraint · priority 1 · high confidence

Set viewpoint and subject distance first. For the same rectilinear viewpoint, focal length primarily changes field of view on a given sensor. Moving to maintain framing changes perspective.

**Why it works.** Relative apparent scale, overlap and parallax follow camera placement. Lens distortion is a separate rendering effect.

**Apply.** Lens selection, matching subject scale and dolly-versus-zoom decisions. **Exception.** Use a fisheye or stylized projection deliberately. Specify the intended distortion in the visual bible.

**Example.** A close wide view exaggerates near-far scale. A farther viewpoint with tighter framing makes near and far apparent sizes more similar. **Common mistake.** Expecting an 85 mm label alone to create a consistent relationship between subject and background.

**LLM action.** Specify viewpoint, subject scale, field of view or full-frame equivalent, and relative background size. Treat numerical lens text as an intent cue in a generator. **Check.** Check relative sizes and parallax against the plan. Confirm that matched framing has the intended perspective.

**Origin.** Projective perspective and the Fujifilm camera-position comparison. [SRC-FUJI-PERS](../sources/bibliography.md#src-fuji-pers) · [SRC-ASC-DIST](../sources/bibliography.md#src-asc-dist) · [SRC-ASC-POS](../sources/bibliography.md#src-asc-pos)

**Related.** [CIN-CAM-002](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-002), [CIN-CAM-008](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-008), [CIN-CAM-009](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-009), [CIN-CAM-013](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-013), [CIN-CAM-015](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-015)

### CIN-CAM-011

**Use focus depth and focus pulls to direct attention** · established technique · priority 2 · high confidence

Choose which distances are acceptably sharp and when the plane of focus should change.

**Why it works.** Selective focus can isolate a subject. Deep focus lets the viewer compare simultaneous actions. A pull can shift emphasis without moving the camera.

**Apply.** Close-ups, reveals and deep staging. **Exception.** A soft or missed focus can express subjectivity. Make clear which fact must still read.

**Example.** Hold the camera and transfer focus from a foreground object to the person beyond it on a sound cue. **Common mistake.** Calling for both very shallow focus and two widely separated faces to remain sharp without a planned technique.

**LLM action.** Specify focus targets, relative depth, order and reveal trigger. Test shallow and deep versions if the model smears details. **Check.** At the intended beat the target is sharp enough to read. Inspect focus transitions for texture drift.

**Origin.** Nikon aperture basics and ACMI focus examples. [SRC-NIK-AP](../sources/bibliography.md#src-nik-ap) · [SRC-ACMI-COMP](../sources/bibliography.md#src-acmi-comp) · [SRC-ASC-CRAFT](../sources/bibliography.md#src-asc-craft)

**Related.** [CIN-CAM-008](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-008), [CIN-CAM-009](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-009), [CIN-CAM-010](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-010), [CIN-CAM-012](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-012), [CIN-CAM-017](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-017)

### CIN-CAM-012

**Pan and tilt around a fixed viewpoint** · established technique · priority 2 · high confidence

Use a pan for horizontal rotation and a tilt for vertical rotation. Keep camera position fixed unless the shot explicitly adds translation.

**Why it works.** Rotation can follow motion or reveal a new area while keeping the same spatial vantage.

**Apply.** Tracking attention across a set or revealing height. **Exception.** Combine rotation and translation when the changing perspective serves the beat. Name both paths.

**Example.** From a locked tripod location pan from the empty doorway to the person who entered off-screen. **Common mistake.** Calling a lateral move a pan when parallax is essential.

**LLM action.** Specify fixed position, start target, rotation direction, reveal trigger and final framing. **Check.** Look for the intended reveal without a sliding camera or unexpected parallax.

**Origin.** ACMI camera-movement descriptions. [SRC-ACMI-MOVE](../sources/bibliography.md#src-acmi-move) · [SRC-ASC-POS](../sources/bibliography.md#src-asc-pos)

**Related.** [CIN-CAM-009](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-009), [CIN-CAM-013](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-013), [CIN-CAM-014](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-014), [CIN-CAM-017](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-017)

### CIN-CAM-013

**Translate the camera with a dolly or track** · established technique · priority 2 · high confidence

Use a push-in or pull-out to change distance, or a lateral track to change position alongside or across action.

**Why it works.** Translation changes perspective and can uncover or hide space through parallax. A push can concentrate attention. A pull can restore context.

**Apply.** Reveals, accompanying movement and a change of emotional distance. **Exception.** A fixed view or optical zoom can preserve viewpoint when changing relative space is undesirable.

**Example.** Track sideways past a foreground column to reveal a second person behind it. **Common mistake.** Combining a constant subject scale with a forward move without a compensating lens or coordinated subject path.

**LLM action.** Specify path in scene space, speed, subject path, end framing and foreground parallax. Add a pause at the reveal if the edit needs it. **Check.** Check foreground and background motion, occlusions and endpoint state.

**Origin.** ACMI movement examples and camera-position optics. [SRC-ACMI-MOVE](../sources/bibliography.md#src-acmi-move) · [SRC-FUJI-PERS](../sources/bibliography.md#src-fuji-pers)

**Related.** [CIN-CAM-009](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-009), [CIN-CAM-010](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-010), [CIN-CAM-012](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-012), [CIN-CAM-014](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-014), [CIN-CAM-015](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-015), [CIN-CAM-017](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-017)

### CIN-CAM-014

**Use a crane or orbit when changing viewpoint reveals space** · established technique · priority 2 · high confidence

A crane moves the viewpoint vertically or through an arc. An orbit moves around a target, typically with rotation to keep it framed.

**Why it works.** A height change can disclose a route or relation. An orbit can expose new sides, change axis, or let the viewer discover the scene progressively.

**Apply.** Spatial reveals and formal long takes. **Exception.** Use a partial arc or a planned cut when geography or identity becomes unstable.

**Example.** Rise above a partition far enough to reveal two people working on opposite sides. **Common mistake.** Prompting a 360-degree orbit with incompatible background maps and unchanged screen directions.

**LLM action.** State origin, destination, radius or height change, target and stop. Track hidden sides, axis crossings and floor-level anchors. **Check.** The move reveals the intended new information and preserves subject and object geometry.

**Origin.** Camera-position mechanics and AFI shot rationale. [SRC-ASC-POS](../sources/bibliography.md#src-asc-pos) · [SRC-ACMI-MOVE](../sources/bibliography.md#src-acmi-move) · [SRC-AFI-DIR](../sources/bibliography.md#src-afi-dir)

### CIN-CAM-015

**Use zoom to change field of view from one position** · technical constraint · priority 2 · high confidence

For a zoom-only shot, keep the camera position fixed and change field of view. An ordinary zoom does not create the parallax of a dolly.

**Why it works.** This isolates enlargement or widening as a formal emphasis. A crash zoom can make the camera's attention conspicuous.

**Apply.** Fast reframing, observation from a fixed position and intentional style. **Exception.** A coordinated dolly zoom deliberately changes distance and focal length to hold one subject scale while changing the background relation.

**Example.** Zoom tightly to isolate an object at the end of a fixed-view shot. **Common mistake.** Prompting a zoom and expecting the near wall to reveal hidden space through a change of viewpoint.

**LLM action.** Specify zoom in or out, fixed position, target scale and pace. Explicitly separate any required dolly move. **Check.** Check subject enlargement and stable relative occlusion for a zoom-only shot.

**Origin.** Focal length and position optics applied to ACMI zoom. [SRC-ACMI-MOVE](../sources/bibliography.md#src-acmi-move) · [SRC-FUJI-PERS](../sources/bibliography.md#src-fuji-pers) · [SRC-ACMI-COMP](../sources/bibliography.md#src-acmi-comp)

### CIN-CAM-016

**Choose locked or handheld behavior deliberately** · established technique · priority 2 · high confidence

A locked frame can let the action and environment define rhythm. Controlled handheld motion can give bodily presence, urgency or observation.

**Why it works.** The stability of the viewpoint changes where movement comes from and how the audience feels it.

**Apply.** Performance-led shots and comparisons of subjective distance. **Exception.** Handheld can be gentle and locked-off can be tense. Choose by the scene's effect.

**Example.** Hold a still frame while small movements become conspicuous. Try restrained handheld when the viewpoint should feel physically present. **Common mistake.** Using random shake as a shortcut to urgency.

**LLM action.** Specify stable horizon and no drift, or controlled movement with amplitude and target framing. **Check.** Watch at normal speed and check that camera motion never hides the key action or violates the continuity plan.

**Origin.** ACMI movement teaching and AFI's shot rationale. [SRC-ACMI-MOVE](../sources/bibliography.md#src-acmi-move) · [SRC-AFI-DIR](../sources/bibliography.md#src-afi-dir) · [SRC-TARK-ESSAY](../sources/bibliography.md#src-tark-essay)

### CIN-CAM-017

**Time movement to a beat and protect the endpoint** · creative heuristic · priority 1 · medium confidence

Give a move a dramatic trigger, a pace, an endpoint and enough usable hold or action overlap to edit.

**Why it works.** A timed reveal or reframing helps the intended beat land. Editorial handles leave room to join clips.

**Apply.** Any moving shot or planned camera shift in a generated clip. **Exception.** Sustained continuous movement can define the whole scene. Locate its change of emphasis and any recovery point.

**Example.** Begin a slow push only after a listener registers a sound, then settle in time to read the response. **Common mistake.** Listing several simultaneous camera moves and expecting timestamps to control every frame.

**LLM action.** Describe start composition, ordered motion, terminal composition and estimated usable duration. Check exact provider timing separately. **Check.** Scrub start, trigger and end, then verify the intended emphasis and cut in the assembled timeline.

**Origin.** AFI rationale for moves and cuts, adapted to generation planning. [SRC-AFI-DIR](../sources/bibliography.md#src-afi-dir) · [SRC-ACMI-MOVE](../sources/bibliography.md#src-acmi-move)

**Related.** [CIN-CAM-011](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-011), [CIN-CAM-012](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-012), [CIN-CAM-013](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-013), [CIN-CAM-014](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-014), [CIN-CAM-015](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-015), [CIN-CAM-016](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-016), [DIR-010](02_directing.md#dir-010)

### CIN-CAM-018

**Use inserts and cutaways to serve the cut** · established technique · priority 2 · high confidence

An insert isolates relevant detail. A cutaway shifts attention to a related person, object or space outside the immediate action view.

**Why it works.** Detail can clarify a cause or hide a join. A cutaway can redirect knowledge or make an elided action understandable.

**Apply.** Props, reactions, reveals and continuity repair. **Exception.** Hold on the action when the viewer should witness it uninterrupted.

**Example.** Use a view of a changed indicator to establish the result of an off-screen manipulation. **Common mistake.** Inserting an attractive detail that promises a role the story never gives it.

**LLM action.** Mark the detail's narrative function, prop state, orientation and audio relationship to neighboring shots. **Check.** Play the sequence without the insert. Keep it if it supplies necessary information, attention or editorial flexibility.

**Origin.** Analytical editing and shot-size practice, adapted to AI coverage. [SRC-ACMI-SHOT](../sources/bibliography.md#src-acmi-shot) · [SRC-SMITH-AT](../sources/bibliography.md#src-smith-at) · [SRC-AFI-DIR](../sources/bibliography.md#src-afi-dir)

## Translate a shot into geometry

1. Name the shot's purpose, focal subject and action. Establish the world coordinate system and identify the actor pair or movement line that forms the current axis.
2. Choose camera position and height, optical axis, field of view or focal-length equivalent, delivery crop, and focus target. Describe what the frame must contain and the relation between layers.
3. Write the subject path separately from the camera path. For camera rotation keep a fixed position. For translation describe the path, endpoint, reveal and parallax. For zoom describe field-of-view change at the chosen position. For focus change identify both depth targets and the trigger.
4. Check for conflicts such as a stationary camera with dolly parallax, a full orbit with unchanged screen direction, or a requested close-up with the entire performer visible at the same moment. Review intermediate frames for identity and geography changes.

For a simple two-person axis, put actor A at `(-1, 0)` and B at `(1, 0)` in plan view. Choose the `y < 0` camera side. Record one establishing frame with both figures, then check the screenward eyelines for the close views from that side. If B crosses the room and changes the dominant line, show the move or establish the new arrangement before using the new reverse. This is a small blocking test.

The camera brief can specify a real optical effect without claiming the generator exposes a calibrated lens. Review relative size, perspective, sharpness and parallax in the result. For narrative interpretation, judge the effect of height, symmetry, negative space or movement in the scene and cut.

# 04 · Mise-en-scène

Mise-en-scène joins the arrangement and performance of people, objects, setting, costume and light within a scene. This chapter focuses on staging and design. The camera chapter specifies viewpoint and the lighting chapter will specify the light. In Russian, мизансцена often emphasizes placement and movement of performers. Keep the scope explicit in production handoffs. [SRC-OSU-MISE](../sources/bibliography.md#src-osu-mise) · [SRC-HSE-STAGE](../sources/bibliography.md#src-hse-stage)

## Rules

### MIS-001

**Map the playable space** · established technique · priority 1 · high confidence

Draw entrances, exits, anchors, obstacles and visibility before positioning cameras or performers.

**Why it works.** A shared map makes routes, sightlines and actions consistent across separately generated angles.

**Apply.** Repeated locations and interactions that depend on geography. **Exception.** An abstract space can have flexible geometry. Identify the relations the story still depends on.

**Example.** Put the witness, a door and an opaque screen on one plan so every view agrees about who can see the action. **Common mistake.** Redesigning the room independently for each shot.

**LLM action.** Give spatial anchors stable IDs, one orientation, relative dimensions and occlusion rules. Preserve them in every scene reference. **Check.** Trace every route, look and camera from the same map.

**Origin.** HSE staging practice and AFI concept modeling. [SRC-HSE-STAGE](../sources/bibliography.md#src-hse-stage) · [SRC-AFI-DES](../sources/bibliography.md#src-afi-des)

**Related.** [MIS-004](04_mise_en_scene.md#mis-004), [MIS-005](04_mise_en_scene.md#mis-005), [MIS-010](04_mise_en_scene.md#mis-010), [MIS-012](04_mise_en_scene.md#mis-012), [CIN-CAM-003](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-003)

### MIS-002

**Use interpersonal distance as action** · established technique · priority 2 · medium confidence

Change distance, angle of approach, access or boundary crossing when it reveals the relationship.

**Why it works.** Relative space can communicate intimacy, caution, avoidance, solidarity or status through action.

**Apply.** Negotiations, reunions and shifts of power. **Exception.** Cultural context and the characters' established behavior can reverse a reading. Use a baseline for this film.

**Example.** One person closes the gap until a second places a bag between them. **Common mistake.** Assigning universal meanings to fixed distances without considering context.

**LLM action.** Record distance trend, orientation, obstacle, trigger and who initiates or resists the move. **Check.** The relation before and after the move is readable even with dialogue muted.

**Origin.** Proxemics and scene staging as interpreted in the rulebook. [SRC-HALL-SPACE](../sources/bibliography.md#src-hall-space) · [SRC-OSU-MISE](../sources/bibliography.md#src-osu-mise) · [SRC-BORD-CROSS](../sources/bibliography.md#src-bord-cross)

### MIS-003

**Place power in the staging** · established technique · priority 2 · medium confidence

Express power through access to the center, height, exits, barriers, seating, group attention or control of an object.

**Why it works.** The audience can read who controls a situation and observe a change without an explanatory line.

**Apply.** Hierarchy, exclusion and reversals. **Exception.** Subvert a familiar image of authority through the response of the others or an ironic relation between set and behavior.

**Example.** A visitor stands at the far end of a table until the seated group turns to hear them. **Common mistake.** Treating centrality or height as proof of authority in every scene.

**LLM action.** Specify who controls access and which spatial change externalizes the turn. Design a clear before and after frame. **Check.** Check what the group does when power changes and whether the image makes that behavior legible.

**Origin.** Bordwell on blocking and Ken Adam's design account. [SRC-BORD-CROSS](../sources/bibliography.md#src-bord-cross) · [SRC-BFI-ADAM](../sources/bibliography.md#src-bfi-adam) · [SRC-HSE-STAGE](../sources/bibliography.md#src-hse-stage)

### MIS-004

**Orient the body and gaze to a target** · established technique · priority 1 · high confidence

Set torso, head, gaze, hands and feet in relation to the action and the other performers.

**Why it works.** Alignment can show attention, concealment, readiness or withdrawal. It also keeps reverse shots coherent.

**Apply.** Dialogue, physical tasks and reference frames. **Exception.** A mismatch can be the intended signal, such as a body preparing to leave while the gaze stays with a speaker.

**Example.** A character faces the door with one foot while maintaining eye contact at the table. **Common mistake.** Asking the character to face the door and look straight into the lens without a consistent pose.

**LLM action.** Record orientation and gaze targets by ID. Use separate constraints for head and torso where useful. **Check.** Check hands, gaze, feet and weight against the action and floor plan at both endpoints.

**Origin.** Bordwell's blocking analysis and actor-oriented performance work. [SRC-BORD-CROSS](../sources/bibliography.md#src-bord-cross) · [SRC-WEST-TOOLS](../sources/bibliography.md#src-west-tools) · [SRC-SMITH-THESIS](../sources/bibliography.md#src-smith-thesis)

### MIS-005

**Stage entrances and exits with consequences** · continuity convention · priority 1 · high confidence

Define the boundary, movement vector, on-screen time, and state before entering and after leaving.

**Why it works.** The viewer can infer route, timing and relations across a cut.

**Apply.** Arrival, departure, pursuit and unseen movement. **Exception.** A sudden entrance can be a surprise. Reconcile the path or preserve ambiguity as intended.

**Example.** A person crosses behind an opaque screen and emerges at the mapped opening with the same object. **Common mistake.** An actor exits screen right and immediately reappears in a location the planned route cannot reach.

**LLM action.** Specify entry or exit point, screen direction, route and who witnesses it. Anchor the geometry in a wider frame if needed. **Check.** Trace the motion across cuts and check what other figures plausibly notice.

**Origin.** Smith's discussion of entrances and exits, applied to staging. [SRC-SMITH-AT](../sources/bibliography.md#src-smith-at) · [SRC-BORD-CROSS](../sources/bibliography.md#src-bord-cross)

**Related.** [MIS-001](04_mise_en_scene.md#mis-001), [MIS-004](04_mise_en_scene.md#mis-004), [MIS-010](04_mise_en_scene.md#mis-010), [MIS-012](04_mise_en_scene.md#mis-012), [CIN-CAM-004](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-004), [CIN-CAM-007](03_cinematography.md#cin-cam-007)

### MIS-006

**Use design to express the world and the situation** · established technique · priority 2 · high confidence

Choose a small number of spatial features, materials and signs of use that express the location's function and pressures.

**Why it works.** The environment can establish a history, routine or power relationship through visible evidence.

**Apply.** New locations and production design for frame generation. **Exception.** Sparse or expressionist design can carry the intention through scale, shape or emptiness.

**Example.** A carefully repaired surface beside an unused ceremonial seat suggests where daily work happens. **Common mistake.** Adding generically luxurious or ruined detail that the scene has no use for.

**LLM action.** Create a location record with fixed anchors, materials, wear, controlled variation and one sentence on dramatic use. **Check.** A viewer can infer the space's use from its visible features. Compare geometry and key materials across angles.

**Origin.** AFI visual development and Adam's account of set design. [SRC-AFI-DES](../sources/bibliography.md#src-afi-des) · [SRC-OSU-MISE](../sources/bibliography.md#src-osu-mise) · [SRC-BFI-ADAM](../sources/bibliography.md#src-bfi-adam)

### MIS-007

**Treat props as stateful action objects** · creative heuristic · priority 1 · high confidence

Identify the props that change action or reveal information. Track holder, location, orientation, use and changed state.

**Why it works.** A prop can motivate behavior and make a consequence visible. A state ledger prevents accidental appearance or reset.

**Apply.** Handoffs, reveals, inserts and repeated use. **Exception.** Decorative set dressing can stay descriptive when it carries no action or cue.

**Example.** Record a document as folded, carried by A, then open in B's hand after the exchange. **Common mistake.** Creating the post-handoff state early or switching the carrying hand across cuts.

**LLM action.** Use stable prop IDs with before and after states, ownership and authorized changes by beat. **Check.** Every important state transition has an observable cause or intentional ellipsis.

**Origin.** Chekhov on emphasis and film design practice, extended into continuity tracking. [SRC-CHEK-GUN](../sources/bibliography.md#src-chek-gun) · [SRC-OSU-MISE](../sources/bibliography.md#src-osu-mise) · [SRC-AFI-DES](../sources/bibliography.md#src-afi-des)

### MIS-008

**Keep costumes legible and continuous** · established technique · priority 2 · high confidence

Specify silhouette, materials, palette, layers, accessories, wear and changes that reveal function or character.

**Why it works.** A stable costume supports identity. A meaningful change can express action, status or time.

**Apply.** Character references, continuity and scenes with costume changes. **Exception.** A disguise, transformation or stylized jump can shift design. Identify what the audience should still recognize.

**Example.** Keep a fixed jacket silhouette while wetness increases after a mapped event. **Common mistake.** Using only a color adjective, then accepting changing collars, closures and accessories.

**LLM action.** Give each look a version and state changes. Use a clean reference from multiple useful angles for important details. **Check.** Compare garment silhouette, texture, closure, accessories and damage or wetness to the reference.

**Origin.** Film studies on costume and AFI visual-development practice. [SRC-OSU-MISE](../sources/bibliography.md#src-osu-mise) · [SRC-AFI-DES](../sources/bibliography.md#src-afi-des) · [SRC-GIKIT-PLAN](../sources/bibliography.md#src-gikit-plan)

**Related.** [MIS-006](04_mise_en_scene.md#mis-006), [MIS-007](04_mise_en_scene.md#mis-007), [MIS-011](04_mise_en_scene.md#mis-011), [MIS-012](04_mise_en_scene.md#mis-012)

### MIS-009

**Make environmental motion interact with action** · established technique · priority 2 · high confidence

Give important wind, rain, machinery or background activity a direction, cause and consequence.

**Why it works.** Environment can reveal a condition, change tactics or establish how the character inhabits the space.

**Apply.** Weather, props, movement in depth and environmental storytelling. **Exception.** Atmospheric movement may serve rhythm or tone even without altering the plot. Keep it subordinate to the beat.

**Example.** Wind moves a loose curtain and briefly reveals a figure in the doorway. **Common mistake.** Generating incompatible wind direction or background activity that upstages the decisive action.

**LLM action.** Specify environmental motion separately from subject and camera motion. Add an interaction and its state change if relevant. **Check.** Inspect direction and continuity across shots and check that the intended reveal remains legible.

**Origin.** Hitchcock on the possibilities of the setting and BFI on Kurosawa's weather. [SRC-HITCH-AFI](../sources/bibliography.md#src-hitch-afi) · [SRC-KURO-WEATH](../sources/bibliography.md#src-kuro-weath) · [SRC-HSE-STAGE](../sources/bibliography.md#src-hse-stage)

### MIS-010

**Use masking and depth to reveal in time** · established technique · priority 2 · high confidence

Use bodies, walls, props or focus to control what is hidden, when it becomes visible, and whose reaction can be read.

**Why it works.** Moving occlusion can reorganize attention within one view without requiring a cut.

**Apply.** Group scenes, surprises and long takes. **Exception.** Keep simultaneous cues visible when the viewer should compare them. Use contrast or timing to manage competition.

**Example.** An actor's step clears a view of a listener just as the listener turns away. **Common mistake.** Allowing a foreground figure to block the only sign of a decisive event.

**LLM action.** Mark layer, occluder, camera and subject paths, reveal beat and visibility before and after. **Check.** Scrub the planned reveal and confirm that all earlier occlusions are intentional.

**Origin.** Bordwell's analysis of actor crossings and staging in depth. [SRC-BORD-CROSS](../sources/bibliography.md#src-bord-cross) · [SRC-OSU-MISE](../sources/bibliography.md#src-osu-mise) · [SRC-HSE-STAGE](../sources/bibliography.md#src-hse-stage)

### MIS-011

**Give movement a playable purpose** · established technique · priority 1 · high confidence

Choose the smallest clearly readable body action that pursues the intention or responds to a change.

**Why it works.** A viewer can read a tactic, relationship or hesitation through behavior. Restrained action can preserve timing and geometry in a clip.

**Apply.** Blocking, silent action and camera-facing performance. **Exception.** Choreographed or decorative movement can become the form itself. Design its rhythm and hierarchy.

**Example.** The performer starts to stand, hears the response and settles back while retaining the object. **Common mistake.** Adding fidgeting, gestures and wandering with no relation to the objective.

**LLM action.** Specify target, movement trigger, path and terminal pose. Let background figures hold or react in order. **Check.** With the scene muted, distinguish meaningful motion from ambient motion and check the terminal pose.

**Origin.** Stanislavski's action, Weston's process tools and the HSE workshop. [SRC-STAN-ACT](../sources/bibliography.md#src-stan-act) · [SRC-WEST-TOOLS](../sources/bibliography.md#src-west-tools) · [SRC-HSE-STAGE](../sources/bibliography.md#src-hse-stage)

### MIS-012

**Carry scene state through the visual bible** · creative heuristic · priority 1 · medium confidence

Freeze the stable attributes of a scene and describe only authorized changes in each shot. Keep a reference for entry and exit state.

**Why it works.** A shared versioned reference gives image generation and editorial review the same anchors.

**Apply.** Any sequence assembled from separate generations. **Exception.** An intended discontinuity needs a distinct branch or version and a cue that helps viewers read the change.

**Example.** A reverse angle references the same set map, costume version and post-handoff prop state as the preceding view. **Common mistake.** Respecifying the whole environment with new wording for every shot.

**LLM action.** Keep an immutable reference block plus a per-beat delta. Store camera and actor state separately and keep source frame IDs. **Check.** Compare the expected and observed state at each join. Reject unintended changes that affect identity, geography or causal action.

**Origin.** Production-design planning, continuity editing, and rulebook adaptation. [SRC-AFI-DES](../sources/bibliography.md#src-afi-des) · [SRC-HSE-STAGE](../sources/bibliography.md#src-hse-stage) · [SRC-SMITH-AT](../sources/bibliography.md#src-smith-at)

## Stage map and visual-bible handoff

| Record | Freeze for the scene | Change on a beat when specified |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Location | Floor plan, coordinates, boundaries, door swing, surfaces, sightlines, permanent objects | Entry or exit, door position, smoke, weather, light source on or off |
| Character | Identity reference, silhouette, costume version, baseline placement | Route, facing, gaze, posture, grip, gesture, garment condition |
| Prop | ID, shape, scale, material and initial position | Holder, hand, orientation, open or closed state, damage and location |
| Attention | Intended first target, depth, competing elements | Reveal, occlusion, change of emphasis or deliberate ambiguity |
| Continuity | Shared axis and state identifiers, cue for each required match | Authorized deltas and the time or shot where they take effect |

Review the planned action without dialogue first. Ask who approaches, controls access, handles the action object, or sees the change. Restore spoken and audible cues and confirm which inference they add. For a space with a planned reveal, draw the path in plan view and verify the reveal in the frame sequence.

Bordwell's staging analysis gives examples of a moving actor changing the picture's emphasis without a cut. Ken Adam's War Room account shows how furniture, geometry, materials and light can be conceived together. Use the staging or set decision that makes the intended relation legible.

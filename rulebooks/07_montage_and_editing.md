# 07 · Montage and editing

A cut can maintain the viewer's spatial and temporal model, redirect attention or create an association. Identify the intended relation before arranging footage. Treat continuity as a convention with a purpose, and evaluate deliberate discontinuities through the audience's experience.

## Rules

### EDT-001

**Orient the viewer through the sequence** · continuity convention · priority 1 · high confidence

Keep the relevant action, direction, time and relationships legible across the sequence. Re-establish after a meaningful change.

**Why it works.** The viewer can follow cause and response without reconstructing irrelevant geometry.

**Apply.** Coverage assemblies, entrances, exits and moving axes. **Exception.** A discontinuity can be expressive when the viewer understands the intended degree of confusion or rupture.

**Example.** A moving character changes the line of action on screen, and the next reverse follows the re-established line. **Common mistake.** Checking that individual frames look plausible without playing the cut.

**LLM action.** Compare screen direction, axes, object state and salient action across all neighboring shots. **Check.** A first viewer can follow who did what, to whom, and when.

**Origin.** Smith on continuity editing and attentional expectations. [SRC-SMITH-AT](../sources/bibliography.md#src-smith-at) · [SRC-SMITH-THESIS](../sources/bibliography.md#src-smith-thesis) · [SRC-ACMI-EDIT](../sources/bibliography.md#src-acmi-edit)

**Related.** `EDT-002`, `EDT-004`, `EDT-008`, `EDT-014`, `CIN-CAM-004`, `MIS-012`

### EDT-002

**Choose the cut phase of a continuous action** · continuity convention · priority 1 · high confidence

Align motion phases and choose the cut point that makes the action read as one continuous event.

**Why it works.** The incoming movement can carry attention and reduce a perceptual interruption.

**Apply.** Two views of one movement and generation joins. **Exception.** A repeated or skipped phase can emphasize an impact or express time loss. Make the repetition or gap deliberate.

**Example.** Select two takes that share the moment before a hand contacts a latch, then trim into one opening. **Common mistake.** Cutting between two identical endpoints so the gesture freezes or repeats.

**LLM action.** Set source in/out and timeline edit point using a named action anchor. Compare one earlier and later cut. **Check.** Single-step for phase and state. Play at speed for the intended motion and sound.

**Origin.** ACMI action cutting and Smith's attention account. [SRC-ACMI-EDIT](../sources/bibliography.md#src-acmi-edit) · [SRC-SMITH-AT](../sources/bibliography.md#src-smith-at)

**Related.** `EDT-001`, `EDT-003`, `EDT-004`, `EDT-009`, `CIN-CAM-007`, `SND-005`

### EDT-003

**Rank emotion, story, and rhythm before cosmetic repair** · creative heuristic · priority 1 · high confidence

Compare candidate cuts for emotional truth, story contribution, rhythm, eye trace, two-dimensional continuity and three-dimensional space. Resolve tradeoffs in that order when it fits the scene.

**Why it works.** The best geometric match can still damage an important reaction. An intentional compromise can protect the central experience.

**Apply.** When several edits satisfy different criteria or generation coverage is incomplete. **Exception.** Geographic or temporal confusion can destroy the story. Treat required comprehension as part of the story criterion.

**Example.** Keep a slightly awkward match if its reaction is essential and the viewer still follows the action. **Common mistake.** Using the six weights as a universal film score or using emotion to excuse incoherence.

**LLM action.** Write which criteria each candidate serves and what is sacrificed. Check comprehension before keeping the compromise. **Check.** Show both versions and ask which better carries the intended feeling and story information.

**Origin.** Walter Murch's Rule of Six. [SRC-MURCH-SIX](../sources/bibliography.md#src-murch-six) · [SRC-SMITH-AT](../sources/bibliography.md#src-smith-at)

**Related.** `EDT-001`, `EDT-002`, `EDT-004`, `EDT-012`, `EDT-014`, `DIR-012`

### EDT-004

**Use rhythm and eye trace to manage attention** · established technique · priority 2 · high confidence

Decide where the audience is likely looking at the outgoing cut, where it should look next, and how long it needs to read the new event.

**Why it works.** Aligning attention can ease a cut. Deliberately shifting it can create a jolt or discovery.

**Apply.** Fast cutting, significant inserts, musical timing and contrast cuts. **Exception.** A search for the new target can be the point. Leave time for that search.

**Example.** Cut from the position of a hand to a new detail in a nearby screen region, then hold for the reaction. **Common mistake.** Cutting every time on a music beat while the visual action remains unreadable.

**LLM action.** Mark focal screen zone, anticipated eye movement and reading time. Compare in context and at delivery size. **Check.** The viewer finds the new action in time. The cut cadence supports the beat rather than obscuring it.

**Origin.** Murch on rhythm and eye trace and Smith's attentional account. [SRC-MURCH-SIX](../sources/bibliography.md#src-murch-six) · [SRC-SMITH-AT](../sources/bibliography.md#src-smith-at) · [SRC-BLOCK-VIS](../sources/bibliography.md#src-block-vis)

**Related.** `EDT-001`, `EDT-003`, `EDT-006`, `EDT-010`, `EDT-012`, `CIN-CAM-008`

### EDT-005

**Use adjacent shots to change an inference** · established technique · priority 1 · high confidence

Pair a reaction, space or gesture with a context shot and test what new meaning the combination suggests.

**Why it works.** Viewers may attribute emotion or causal relation partly from context.

**Apply.** Neutral reactions, off-screen discoveries and ambiguous images. **Exception.** A performance with strong specific information can overpower a context cue. Deliberate ambiguity can invite several readings.

**Example.** Hold the same neutral reaction in two edit versions and change the preceding object to test the perceived emotion. **Common mistake.** Assuming any neutral face will produce the same response in all viewers.

**LLM action.** Use the same reaction asset and vary only the context when testing. Record intended inference and alternate readings. **Check.** Show a short A/B cut and ask viewers to name the emotion or event and the cue they used.

**Origin.** Kuleshov tradition and modern bounded replication. [SRC-KUL-TEST](../sources/bibliography.md#src-kul-test) · [SRC-SMITH-AT](../sources/bibliography.md#src-smith-at) · [SRC-EIS-FORM](../sources/bibliography.md#src-eis-form)

**Related.** `EDT-004`, `EDT-006`, `EDT-007`, `EDT-014`, `DIR-007`

### EDT-006

**Specify the relation a montage creates** · established technique · priority 2 · high confidence

Use shot duration, movement, dominant affect, interacting qualities or conceptual collision to build a planned relation across shots.

**Why it works.** An edit sequence can create a comparison or idea the individual images do not express alone.

**Apply.** Associative sequences, emotional pressure and graphic or intellectual contrast. **Exception.** Continuity or a long hold can serve when the event must be witnessed in one spatial relation.

**Example.** Cut between a repeated machine action and a repeated human action to invite comparison. **Common mistake.** Calling a collection of unrelated attractive shots montage and relying on the audience to invent the intended relation.

**LLM action.** Choose the primary relation, shot pairs, motif and planned change. Try a different order and compare its implication. **Check.** The viewer can articulate a plausible relation that serves the theme or event.

**Origin.** Eisenstein's Methods of Montage and Pudovkin's discussion of contrast. [SRC-EIS-FORM](../sources/bibliography.md#src-eis-form) · [SRC-PUD-FILM](../sources/bibliography.md#src-pud-film) · [SRC-EIS-BFI](../sources/bibliography.md#src-eis-bfi)

**Related.** `EDT-004`, `EDT-005`, `EDT-007`, `EDT-010`, `EDT-012`, `EDT-013`

### EDT-007

**Build a sequence by contrast or constructive selection** · established technique · priority 2 · high confidence

Select details that let the viewer reconstruct an event or compare two actions. Give each shot an inferential role.

**Why it works.** Reordering or contrasting selected views can clarify cause, build parallel meaning or strengthen an emotional relation.

**Apply.** Analytical breakdown and association across separate locations. **Exception.** Preserve a continuous take when the evidence or experience depends on witnessing the whole event.

**Example.** Show a change in a mechanism and the resulting reaction in a nearby space, with a cue connecting the two. **Common mistake.** Using a cutaway that implies the wrong event or timing.

**LLM action.** Label each shot as cause, evidence, reaction, contrast or consequence. Assemble a minimally sufficient sequence first. **Check.** A reader can reconstruct the intended event or comparison without relying on an unshown decisive fact.

**Origin.** Pudovkin's own discussion and Cameron's analysis. [SRC-PUD-FILM](../sources/bibliography.md#src-pud-film) · [SRC-PUD-STUDY](../sources/bibliography.md#src-pud-study) · [SRC-ACMI-EDIT](../sources/bibliography.md#src-acmi-edit)

**Related.** `EDT-005`, `EDT-006`, `EDT-008`, `EDT-009`, `EDT-013`, `CIN-CAM-018`

### EDT-008

**Define the time relation in intercut strands** · established technique · priority 2 · high confidence

When alternating strands, specify whether they are simultaneous, causally ordered, or juxtaposed for a comparison. Build an anchor for the required reading.

**Why it works.** The alternation can build anticipation or theme without accidentally implying that every event happens together.

**Apply.** Cross-cut rescue or pursuit, parallel routines and different timelines. **Exception.** Leave temporal relation ambiguous when the audience benefits from comparing actions without placing them on one clock.

**Example.** Use a shared audible signal or event to indicate that two actions occur together. **Common mistake.** Intercutting unrelated actions with a countdown and accidentally promising that they will meet.

**LLM action.** Track strand IDs, story order, concurrent cues and intended inference. Preserve separate location and audio state. **Check.** Ask a viewer what is simultaneous and what is thematic. Confirm the answer fits the edit's intention.

**Origin.** Pudovkin's parallel and simultaneous examples, applied to editing. [SRC-PUD-FILM](../sources/bibliography.md#src-pud-film) · [SRC-PUD-STUDY](../sources/bibliography.md#src-pud-study)

**Related.** `EDT-001`, `EDT-005`, `EDT-006`, `EDT-007`, `EDT-009`

### EDT-009

**Use ellipsis to compress time** · established technique · priority 1 · high confidence

Omit intervals that viewers can reconstruct. Give the next shot a readable change of state or time marker.

**Why it works.** The film keeps pace while preserving causality and emotional duration where it matters.

**Apply.** Routine travel, repeated tasks and changes in time. **Exception.** Show elapsed effort when duration, labor or waiting is the experience. A compressed version may lose the point.

**Example.** Jump from an early stage of work to a changed state with a recognizable object and a time cue. **Common mistake.** Removing the cause of a consequence and calling the gap economy.

**LLM action.** Mark skipped interval, preserved anchors, changed state and a cue. Test whether the viewer needs an intermediate beat. **Check.** A first viewer understands the time jump, the causal relation and the consequence.

**Origin.** ACMI's condensing-time lesson and Aristotle's plot unity. [SRC-ACMI-EDIT](../sources/bibliography.md#src-acmi-edit) · [SRC-ARIS-POE](../sources/bibliography.md#src-aris-poe)

**Related.** `EDT-001`, `EDT-002`, `EDT-008`, `EDT-011`, `DRA-015`

### EDT-010

**Use graphic or sonic matches with a reason** · established technique · priority 2 · high confidence

Match a visual feature, gesture, rhythm or sound across the cut when similarity or contrast should connect separate images.

**Why it works.** A match can bridge time, place or idea, and can make a discontinuity readable as a designed transition.

**Apply.** Motif returns, ellipsis and expressive transitions. **Exception.** Break the match when difference or rupture carries more force.

**Example.** The cut from the thrown bone to an object in orbit in 2001 uses a graphic relation across a large time gap. **Common mistake.** Matching a color or shape so closely that viewers think a discontinuous location is the same space unintentionally.

**LLM action.** Record shared shape, screen position or sound timing and the intended change in time, space or meaning. **Check.** The matching feature is perceptible at speed and the viewer can infer the intended transition.

**Origin.** Film education examples of graphic matching and sound-bridge practice. [SRC-BPB-MATCH](../sources/bibliography.md#src-bpb-match) · [SRC-ACMI-EDIT](../sources/bibliography.md#src-acmi-edit) · [SRC-ADOBE-JL](../sources/bibliography.md#src-adobe-jl)

**Related.** `EDT-004`, `EDT-006`, `EDT-008`, `EDT-009`, `EDT-014`, `SND-009`

### EDT-011

**Make a jump cut deliberate** · established technique · priority 2 · high confidence

Expose a discontinuity between similar views when a visible jump should express compression, fracture, humor or force.

**Why it works.** The apparent skipping makes the edit itself part of the experience.

**Apply.** Elliptical routines, disruptions and expressive rhythm. **Exception.** Use more distinct coverage, a matched action, a cutaway or a hold when the jump distracts from the scene.

**Example.** Keep the composition stable while repeated intervals skip forward to compress a task. **Common mistake.** Retaining a tiny continuity error because a jump cut was possible in principle.

**LLM action.** Name the skipped interval and intended effect. Test a smoother alternative and record the chosen reason. **Check.** The viewer experiences an intentional jolt and can follow the event to the degree required.

**Origin.** ACMI's jump-cut lesson and BFI's Breathless example. [SRC-ACMI-EDIT](../sources/bibliography.md#src-acmi-edit) · [SRC-BFI-BREATH](../sources/bibliography.md#src-bfi-breath)

**Related.** `EDT-002`, `EDT-004`, `EDT-009`, `EDT-012`, `CIN-CAM-006`

### EDT-012

**Let time accumulate within the shot** · creative heuristic · priority 2 · medium confidence

Try holding through a complete behavioral, spatial or sonic change. Cut when attention or experience benefits from the change of view.

**Why it works.** A duration can give the viewer time to discover an event or inhabit a wait. Cutting quickly may remove that experience.

**Apply.** Observation, suspense, waiting, performance and space. **Exception.** Compress when the viewer already has what the hold can offer or when a cut creates the necessary relation.

**Example.** Hold on an open doorway while a sound recedes and the viewer reinterprets the empty space. **Common mistake.** Confusing a long generated file with a shot that earns its screen time.

**LLM action.** Test a held and edited version using the same dramatic cue. Log the usable generation window and fallback cut point. **Check.** The duration sustains or transforms attention. Ask what would be lost if cut earlier.

**Origin.** Tarkovsky's observation and recorded time, in dialogue with Murch's timing concern. [SRC-TARK-ESSAY](../sources/bibliography.md#src-tark-essay) · [SRC-TARK-TIME](../sources/bibliography.md#src-tark-time) · [SRC-MURCH-SIX](../sources/bibliography.md#src-murch-six)

**Related.** `EDT-003`, `EDT-004`, `EDT-006`, `EDT-011`, `EDT-013`, `DIR-012`

### EDT-013

**Organize observed fragments into a viewpoint** · established technique · priority 3 · medium confidence

Group observed actions, processes or textures around a pattern the sequence can reveal.

**Why it works.** An arrangement can show relationships or rhythms without a conventional protagonist or explanation.

**Apply.** Essayistic, documentary-style and experimental shorts. **Exception.** If the short promises a causal story, preserve the chain of information and decisions along with the pattern.

**Example.** Alternate repeated gestures across people and locations to make a shared routine visible. **Common mistake.** Presenting synthetic footage as documentary evidence.

**LLM action.** Specify motif, image source, perspective, and the progression in what viewers can infer. Label generated or reconstructed assets in the provenance record. **Check.** The sequence conveys a discoverable relation, and its source and presentation agree with the project's disclosure.

**Origin.** Vertov's Kino-Eye tradition and the BFI film account. [SRC-VERT-LEF](../sources/bibliography.md#src-vert-lef) · [SRC-VERT-KINO](../sources/bibliography.md#src-vert-kino) · [SRC-BFI-VERT](../sources/bibliography.md#src-bfi-vert)

**Related.** `EDT-005`, `EDT-006`, `EDT-007`, `EDT-012`, `DRA-016`

### EDT-014

**Carry the emotional state into the next scene** · creative heuristic · priority 1 · high confidence

Design the last cue of one scene and the first cue of the next as one transition. Decide what carries over and what changes.

**Why it works.** A shared emotion, sound or contrast helps the viewer connect scenes even when place or time shifts.

**Apply.** Scene transitions, endings and tonal shifts. **Exception.** An abrupt reset can be an intentional rupture. State what the audience should carry despite it.

**Example.** Let a question hang over the next scene's first image before the next action answers it indirectly. **Common mistake.** Using a transition effect that erases the reaction or suggests an unintended time relation.

**LLM action.** Record exit state, entry state, bridge or contrast, picture boundary and sound overlaps. **Check.** Play the transition without notes and ask what emotion or information crosses the boundary.

**Origin.** Murch's emotion and story priorities plus split-edit practice. [SRC-MURCH-SIX](../sources/bibliography.md#src-murch-six) · [SRC-ADOBE-JL](../sources/bibliography.md#src-adobe-jl) · [SRC-ACMI-EDIT](../sources/bibliography.md#src-acmi-edit)

**Related.** `EDT-001`, `EDT-003`, `EDT-005`, `EDT-008`, `EDT-010`, `SND-009`, `DRA-017`

## Compare the editing traditions

| Lens | Question to ask of the cut | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Continuity and attention | Can the viewer follow the necessary action, timing and geography while changing viewpoint? | [Smith](../sources/bibliography.md#src-smith-at) |
| Kuleshov | What context makes the same look or reaction read differently? | [Replication study](../sources/bibliography.md#src-kul-test) |
| Eisenstein | What tension or relation emerges from durations, movements, tones or ideas? | [Methods of Montage](../sources/bibliography.md#src-eis-form) |
| Pudovkin | What can the viewer reconstruct or compare from selected and ordered details? | [Primary selections](../sources/bibliography.md#src-pud-film) · [Cameron](../sources/bibliography.md#src-pud-study) |
| Vertov | What rhythm or relation becomes visible when observed fragments are reorganized? | [Киноки. Переворот](../sources/bibliography.md#src-vert-lef) · [BFI case](../sources/bibliography.md#src-bfi-vert) |
| Tarkovsky | What experience becomes available if the action or time is allowed to unfold within one view? | [Запечатленное время](../sources/bibliography.md#src-tark-essay) |
| Murch | Which cut best serves emotion, story, rhythm, eye trace, flat-screen continuity and three-dimensional space? | [Rule of Six excerpt](../sources/bibliography.md#src-murch-six) |

Eisenstein's five categories are historical concepts. **Metric** emphasizes measured shot lengths. **Rhythmic** relates duration to movement and content. **Tonal** emphasizes a dominant expressive quality. **Overtonal** considers interacting qualities and tensions. **Intellectual** asks what idea a relation between images produces. One sequence can combine these aspects. The labels are useful when they change a practical editing choice. [SRC-EIS-FORM](../sources/bibliography.md#src-eis-form)

The original Kuleshov footage is reported lost. Barratt and colleagues found context-related differences in ratings under their study conditions, with earlier replication challenges and no expected differentiation across emotion conditions in the eye-tracking results. Use the technique as an A/B edit test, with the reaction held constant. [SRC-KUL-TEST](../sources/bibliography.md#src-kul-test)

## Assemble and compare

Build the rough cut from the dramatic beats. Add only the orientations, reactions, contrasts and sound bridges necessary to test it. Give each cut a purpose and compare candidate points in context. Keep source trims and editorial handles in the shot and audio records. Review emotion, comprehension and duration before addressing cosmetic seams. Verify intended graphic and sonic relations in the delivery crop and mix.

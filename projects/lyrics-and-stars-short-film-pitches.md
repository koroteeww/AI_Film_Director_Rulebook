# Five short film pitches

These are revised pitches for selection. Each includes a film brief, a timed outline and an excerpt from the scene that tests its central choice.

**From the author.** The eight lyric drafts, Vladimir Megre’s excerpts featuring Anastasia, the night-sky scene from *Harry Potter and the Methods of Rationality*, and the 3–7 minute range for shorts or music clips.

**Proposed for this draft.** The characters, situations, endings and song placements below. The target cuts include a short credits reserve. Aspect ratio and music timing are open until a pitch and recording are selected.

## 01 The Key Under the Lilac

**Logline.** On the morning he hands over his house, a widower starts digging up the white lilac he planted with his wife. Then the new owner’s little girl whistles the tune his wife used to call him home.

**Film brief.** Anton wants the original bush in the moving van. He and Leila planted it when they first made a home here. Leaving it means trusting a new family with something he has kept alive for years. The question is what he’ll take with him when the van pulls away. A warm, mostly wordless video, told through a handful of returns to the same gate.

**From the sources.** “Build a home” gives the couple a place they make together, the white lilac and a whistle that returns after the song has lived through years. In Anastasia’s Space of Love, tending living things creates a place capable of giving something back. Leila shares a cutting early enough for her gesture to reach Anton decades later.

### Timed outline

- **BT-001 · 0:00–0:45.** Leila and Anton plant the lilac by the gate. Years later, its branches flower over the latch. Leila whistles Anton in and gives Nika, the neighbor’s child, a cutting in a chipped pot. Nika tries the tune as she carries the pot away, her fourth note a little high.
- **BT-002 · 0:45–1:55.** The house is empty. Anton wraps a photo of him and Leila under the lilac and lays it in a moving box. He tags the bush for the van and digs around it. The roots run under the path, and the driver sets the van ramp down behind him.
- **BT-003 · 1:55–3:25.** A child waiting at the gate whistles. Anton sees the grown Nika behind her, carrying the chipped pot with a new cutting from the one Leila gave her. He pulls off the moving tag, fills the trench back in and gives the girl the garden key.
- **BT-004 · 3:25–4:00.** Nika puts the cutting in the van in place of the tagged bush. As the van pulls away, the girl whistles from inside the garden. Anton answers. Reserve 0:10 for credits.

### Screenplay excerpt

**P01-SC-01 · BT-003 · about 35 seconds**

**EXT. GARDEN GATE – MORNING**

A root cracks under Anton’s spade. He stops with his boot still on the blade.

Someone whistles from the street. The first three notes are Leila’s. The fourth goes a little high.

Anton looks up. A girl leans against the bars. Behind her, Nika holds the chipped pot.

He looks down at the exposed root. He lifts the spade and pushes the loose earth back into the trench.

Anton unlocks the gate. The girl steps back. He puts the key in her open palm.

**Directorial intent.** Let the wrong fourth note identify a tune passed between people. The first time we hear it, Leila is calling someone into the garden. The last time, Anton can answer from outside it. Hold the returning whistle for the song’s outro.

**Rule decisions.** [DRA-007](../rulebooks/01_dramaturgy.md#dra-007) pairs the tagged bush with the handover. [DRA-008](../rulebooks/01_dramaturgy.md#dra-008) gives the cutting and whistle a second function after the time jump.

## 02 The Night We Borrowed

**Logline.** On the night she leaves for college, a girl talks her neighbors into fifteen minutes of darkness so she can show her little brother a star they can both look for when they’re apart. The shopkeeper keeps the last light on for his father, who needs it to see the steps.

**Film brief.** Mira has promised her brother Samir a specific star before her bus leaves. He wants her to show him where to look, and she needs the shopkeeper’s consent to dim the last source of glare. The test is whether she can make room for both the boy looking up and the man looking down. An intimate night story with a small public undertaking.

**From the sources.** In the supplied HPMOR passage, Harry imagines a city with the lights out for fifteen minutes, then uses the privacy of a dark courtyard to show Draco he means what he says. Samir asks Mira to make a promise visible, and the shopkeeper asks her to make a path visible. “Believe” carries the shared sky and the separation. “My Earth EDM” gives weight to the ground under their feet, which becomes part of what they have to care for together.

### Timed outline

- **BT-005 · 0:00–0:45.** Mira tests a rechargeable clip lamp and packs it in her college bag. Samir stops her zipping the bag until she draws a little star chart on each of two scraps of paper and mark the star she promised him. He takes his copy to the courtyard and holds it against a sky washed pale by the shop sign.
- **BT-006 · 0:45–2:05.** The siblings ask for fifteen minutes at each landing. Windows darken. Mira reaches the shop last and sees its owner’s father stop at the top step as the owner is closing the shop. He uses the sign’s glow to find the step edge. The owner shows Mira why the sign stays on until his father gets home.
- **BT-007 · 2:05–4:05.** A bus reminder lights Mira’s phone. She opens her bag, clips her lamp under the awning and aims it down. The shopkeeper switches the sign off to try it. His father walks the steps, stops, and asks them to aim the lamp farther out so it reaches the curb. Mira adjusts it and leaves it clipped in place.
- **BT-008 · 4:05–4:50.** Samir finds the star and points it out to his sister. Mira turns her scrap until it lines up with his. In the departing bus, she holds it to the window. Back in the courtyard, the lamp still lights the steps. Reserve 0:10 for credits.

### Screenplay excerpt

**P02-SC-01 · BT-007 · about 40 seconds**

**EXT. COURTYARD SHOP – NIGHT**

Mira clips the lamp to the awning and turns it on. The shopkeeper looks at his father.

**FATHER**  
Go on.

The shop sign goes dark. The old man walks down one step, then another. At the bottom, his shoe stops short of the curb.

**FATHER**  
A little farther out.

Mira tilts the lamp. The curb comes into view. The old man steps down.

Across the courtyard, Samir lifts his chart toward the sky. Mira takes her hand off the lamp.

**Directorial intent.** Keep the lit steps and the dark sky in the same geography. Let Samir be the one who finds the star after Mira makes the view possible. “Believe” can enter after he points it out.

**Rule decisions.** [DRA-002](../rulebooks/01_dramaturgy.md#dra-002) sets up the lamp in the packing scene and makes its use answer the refusal. [DRA-006](../rulebooks/01_dramaturgy.md#dra-006) keeps the promise measurable through the star on Samir’s scrap.

## 03 The Second Passenger

**Logline.** Minutes before evacuating an orbital greenhouse, a botanist loads five years of living experiments into his shuttle’s only powered bay. Then the crystal he marked for disposal starts answering his knocks with a rhythm of its own.

**Film brief.** Ayan means to bring his living cultures home. Their tray and the crystal’s cradle both need the shuttle’s one cargo power connection during the trip. The question is how much evidence of another mind he needs before he gives up the tray. The form is a first-contact thriller that narrows to two listeners and one socket.

**From the sources.** HPMOR asks how we would recognize a person made of something unfamiliar. It pairs a claim about who deserves care with an experiment that changes someone’s mind. “Quantum Pulse (Stadium Synth-Rock Version)” hears rhythm in matter. Megre’s *Co-Creation* joins different energies into something they could only make together. Ayan has spent years growing one kind of life. The first thing he makes with the crystal is a rhythm, and he has to act while he still has questions about who is answering.

### Timed outline

- **BT-009 · 0:00–0:50.** Ayan locks his plant tray into the shuttle bay and snaps in the cargo power lead. Back at the bench, the same three knocks repeat from the crystal cradle. He tags it for disposal and sets it beside the hatch with the other things staying behind. The evacuation timer starts its final countdown.
- **BT-010 · 0:50–2:35.** Ayan taps an uneven pattern. The crystal fills its gap. He sets the cradle on a foam pad to separate it from the vibrating bench. He stops tapping. After a pause, a different rhythm comes through the cradle. He answers it. The greenhouse power cuts and the cradle switches to its fading reserve.
- **BT-011 · 2:35–5:15.** Ayan carries the cradle to the shuttle and searches the bay for a second lead. There is one connector. He starts to leave the cradle by the hatch. The crystal begins their new rhythm, then falls quiet before its last beat. Ayan removes the plant tray and plugs the cradle in. He closes the hatch with the tray still on the station side.
- **BT-012 · 5:15–6:10.** In transit, the manifest shows one passenger. Ayan changes the count to two and sits beside the bay. He taps the missing beat. After a pause, a new phrase begins. Reserve 0:10 for credits.

### Screenplay excerpt

**P03-SC-01 · BT-011 · about 45 seconds**

**INT. SHUTTLE CARGO BAY – EVACUATION**

Ayan holds the cradle’s plug next to the one feeding the plant tray. Both have the same green stripe.

The cradle knocks twice. Ayan waits for the third. The reserve light winks out.

He unlatches the plant tray. It sticks. He yanks it loose and slides it out onto the greenhouse floor. Leaves catch under the hatch rail. He tucks them inside the tray.

He seats the cradle. The plug clicks. The reserve indicator lights up again.

Ayan presses CLOSE. The tray disappears behind the hatch.

**Directorial intent.** Give the plant tray enough texture and handling to feel like a living loss. Hear the first exchange before the synth-rock enters. Leave a full pause before the last reply, so the audience has time to wait with Ayan.

**Rule decisions.** [DRA-004](../rulebooks/01_dramaturgy.md#dra-004) takes the inquiry from a free experiment to a choice with a cost. [DRA-010](../rulebooks/01_dramaturgy.md#dra-010) shares the evidence with the viewer and leaves its interpretation open.

## 04 The First Quiet Bar

**Logline.** A singer has one take to win her band a festival slot with a song about birds flying free. Halfway to the chorus, a real bird starts hitting the glass, and helping it means stopping the audition in front of the judges.

**Film brief.** Sara wants her band’s first festival booking. She has built their live audition around clean vocals and a loop of birdsong. During the take she has to choose whether to keep singing or get everyone still enough to give the bird a way out. The audition gives way to a performance the band can only make together. Bright pop, with a break in the music at the point where it usually gets loud.

**From the sources.** “Let Goodness Be (Viral Earworm Version)” puts the freedom of birds in its promise and gives its final chorus to a crowd. The scene puts the promise within reach of the singer’s own hands. Anastasia’s attention to a living thing most people would miss gives the silence its focus. “The good will prevail EDM” could put the drop after the bird has flown in an alternate cut.

### Timed outline

- **BT-013 · 0:00–0:40.** A monitor shows the festival panel. The operator signals one take. Sara mutes the room mic and asks her band for quiet. She starts the backing track. The same recorded call opens each bar. Her drummer lifts his sticks and waits for his cue.
- **BT-014 · 0:40–1:30.** A thud breaks the pattern. Sara keeps singing through it until a shadow crosses her music stand and the bird strikes the high window again. It clings to the sill and spreads its wings. She sees daylight under the door that leads outside. The track is building to the chorus.
- **BT-015 · 1:30–2:40.** Sara ends the live take and stops playback. She opens the outside door and draws the band back from it. A chair scrapes. Everyone stops moving. The bird drops from the sill, pauses on the floor and flies through the opening.
- **BT-016 · 2:40–3:30.** Sara taps the beat on the open door. Her drummer picks it up and the band joins her chorus one voice at a time. The audition monitor stays dark. After the last note, Sara holds the door open for another breath. The room can hear the street. Reserve 0:10 for credits.

### Screenplay excerpt

**P04-SC-01 · BT-015 · about 45 seconds**

**INT. REHEARSAL HALL – DAY**

Sara opens her mouth for the chorus. A wing hits the glass.

She taps END TAKE. The panel vanishes from the monitor. She kills the track mid-bar.

At the outside door, Sara leans on the push bar. Daylight spreads across the floor.

The drummer starts to cross to her. Sara holds up a palm. The drummer stops, one stick still in the air.

A flutter. The bird drops to the floor. Sara keeps the door open and lowers her hand.

The bird flies past her into the light.

**Directorial intent.** Let the first wingbeat be easy to mistake for the loop. Leave the room quiet long enough for the release to become a shared action. The next chorus belongs to the people Sara asked to be quiet.

**Rule decisions.** [DRA-005](../rulebooks/01_dramaturgy.md#dra-005) turns the audition when Sara ends it herself. [DRA-014](../rulebooks/01_dramaturgy.md#dra-014) makes the hush something the band does together.

## 05 A Cup With No Name

**Logline.** On her last morning behind the counter, a tea-room keeper tries to hide that she’s forgetting her regulars’ names. Then a man she takes for a stranger orders two cups exactly the way she takes hers, right down to the apricot jam.

**Film brief.** Roza wants to finish the handover and leave before anyone has to supply a name she once knew. She can get most of the way through the morning by serving orders from habit. A regular named Kamal offers her the seat she’s always kept for an extra guest. Sitting down gives her the chance to ask the question she has spent the morning avoiding. A quiet devotional short in one room, with room for embarrassment as well as tenderness.

**From the sources.** “God You remember me” asks what it feels like to be known when you’re losing your hold on who you are. Anastasia’s account of the divine in ordinary people and Megre’s dawn that touches everyone give the scene its scale. “About GOD EDM” could support a second arrangement where the regulars pass the tea round as dawn reaches the whole table.

### Timed outline

- **BT-017 · 0:00–0:55.** Before opening, Roza sets a spare place and takes her tea from a chipped blue cup with apricot jam on the side. She wraps the cup, boxes it and lays the key by the incoming keeper’s ledger. After a knock at the glass, she opens up.
- **BT-018 · 0:55–2:25.** She puts a regular’s cup down as he arrives. He greets her by name. She covers her pause by refilling the sugar. When Kamal enters, she hands him a menu. He takes his usual seat and orders two teas with apricot jam. Roza reaches for the box before catching herself.
- **BT-019 · 2:25–4:30.** Kamal pulls the spare chair out. Roza has her coat on and a clear path to the door. She takes the blue cup out of its wrapping and brings both teas over. She sits down and asks his name. He gives it to her and moves the jam within reach of both of them.
- **BT-020 · 4:30–5:20.** Dawn reaches the table. The incoming keeper picks up the key and takes a new customer’s order. Roza sees the customer pause with a cup and nudges a spare chair out with her foot, still sitting beside Kamal. Reserve 0:10 for credits.

### Screenplay excerpt

**P05-SC-01 · BT-019 · about 45 seconds**

**INT. TEA ROOM – DAWN**

Roza stands with her coat buttoned. The blue cup is still wrapped in the box.

Kamal draws the spare chair out a few inches. He waits.

She unpacks the cup and puts it on a tray with the other tea. The spoon rattles against the saucer as she carries the tray over.

Roza sits.

**ROZA**  
Will you tell me your name?

**KAMAL**  
Kamal.

He slides the jam halfway across the table. Roza turns the spoon so he can reach it, too.

**Directorial intent.** Hold on the question long enough to feel what it costs Roza to ask. The cue from “God You remember me” can enter after he gives his name. Let dawn reach both of them while the room carries on around them.

**Rule decisions.** [DRA-012](../rulebooks/01_dramaturgy.md#dra-012) puts Roza’s concealment in the work she finds to do with her hands. [DRA-017](../rulebooks/01_dramaturgy.md#dra-017) shows the consequence when she can welcome someone while remaining seated.

## Revision notes

- Read P01 and P04 with the dialogue muted. Check whether Anton’s change of mind and the end of Sara’s audition are both visible.
- Time the pauses in P02, P03 and P05. Give the father’s step, the missing beat and Roza’s question enough space for the audience to anticipate an answer.
- Set the music and credits timing against the actual recording once a pitch is selected.

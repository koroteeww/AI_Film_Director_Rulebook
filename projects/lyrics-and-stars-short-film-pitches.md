## 01 The Key Under the Lilac

**Logline.** A widowed gardener keeps the gate to his late partner’s garden locked. When a young neighbor brings back a cutting from their white lilac, he has to decide whether to accept it through the bars or hand her the key.

- `BT-001` · 0:00–0:45. Anton and Leila put up a garden gate. Leila hangs a spare key under the lilac and gives Nika, the girl next door, a cutting in a chipped pot. Nika whistles along with Leila while they work.
- `BT-002` · 0:45–2:05. The gate marks the years. Small hands grow into working hands, the yard fills with chairs, and eventually Anton is the only one bringing tea outside. He takes the spare key indoors. After a storm breaks the old lilac, he props up the split trunk by himself.
- `BT-003` · 2:05–3:20. Nika returns as an adult with a young plant grown from the cutting. Anton reaches through the bars for the pot. She sets it down on her side and holds up a spade. After a long look at the damaged shrub, he goes in for the key and puts it in her hand.
- `BT-004` · 3:20–4:10. They plant it together. In a later spring, Anton sets out a second cup as the gate clicks open. Nika picks up Leila’s whistle. He supplies the last note.

**Image and sound.** Return to the same latch, chipped pot and tea table as the years pass. Start the song with the whistle as an everyday sound. Let the bridge sit with Anton in the quiet yard, then bring the whistle back through another person.

**Source connection.** “Build a home” supplies the native soil, white lilac, aging together and whistle reprise. Anastasia’s Space of Love gives the garden its purpose. The choice to hand over the key shows how that love can outlive the couple.

## 02 The Night We Borrowed

**Logline.** On the night before her family moves, Mira promises her little brother one last look at the stars from their courtyard. The shop sign washes out their view, and the shopkeeper leaves it on all night so people can see the steps.

- `BT-005` · 0:00–0:55. In an apartment full of packed boxes, Mira pulls a star chart from the last box to be taped. Her brother has drawn the courtyard on the back, with Vega just above the roofline. Outside, he points his phone at that patch of sky. In the picture, the roof glows pink from the shop sign and the sky is blank.
- `BT-006` · 0:55–2:25. Mira asks the shopkeeper to turn the sign off. He points out an older customer feeling for the bottom step. Mira tries a darker corner with her brother, but a wall cuts off the view he wanted.
- `BT-007` · 2:25–4:10. Mira goes back and asks the shopkeeper to show her which part of the path he needs lit. At closing, he takes the clip lamp from his counter and aims it down at the steps. Mira walks the path to see where the light falls. He turns the sign off and joins the children in the courtyard.
- `BT-008` · 4:10–5:00. A few stars become visible above the roofline. The brother finds Vega on his chart, then notices the shopkeeper looking in the wrong direction. He steps closer and guides his gaze. Mira lowers her phone and looks up.

**Image and sound.** Keep the courtyard recognizable before and after the sign goes dark. Let the shop’s hum disappear and hold on the three listeners for a beat. A passage from “Believe” can enter once the brother starts pointing things out.

**Source connection.** The supplied *Harry Potter and the Methods of Rationality* scene gives the city-light problem and the intimacy of looking up together. “Believe” contributes the shared sky and letting a moment pass. “My Earth EDM” informs the textures underfoot and the feeling of this ordinary courtyard as part of home.

## 03 The Second Passenger

**Logline.** During an evacuation from an orbital greenhouse, a researcher must decide whether to use the shuttle’s only powered cargo bay for five years of living experiments or for a crystal that has begun to answer him.

- `BT-009` · 0:00–0:55. Ayan straps his plant cultures into the bay and checks the power connection. A shutdown timer starts. Three knocks repeat from a crystal cassette on the neighboring bench. He marks the sound as interference and goes back to packing.
- `BT-010` · 0:55–2:25. Ayan taps an irregular pattern on the bench. The cassette replies after a pause. He changes the pattern twice, then stops. This time the crystal supplies a new rhythm and waits. Ayan answers it. The exchange ends as the bench power shuts down.
- `BT-011` · 2:25–5:20. On reserve power, the crystal knocks once more as its light fades. The shuttle is ready to seal and its single bay can sustain either load. Ayan takes a few dry seeds from his kit, removes the tray of living cultures, and connects the cassette in its place. The station door closes on the work he came to save.
- `BT-012` · 5:20–6:20. During departure, Ayan sits by the bay. The same rhythm arrives through its metal frame. He starts to repeat his test, then taps a new phrase and waits for an answer.

**Image and sound.** Use the same bay for both possible cargoes so the trade is visible. Let the knocks and gaps be audible before the synth-rock enters. The last reply should feel like the beginning of a conversation. Ayan acts on the evidence available to him, with the crystal’s inner life still open.

**Source connection.** The fanfiction excerpt asks how people might recognize a mind made of something unfamiliar. “Quantum Pulse (Stadium Synth-Rock Version)” turns a small rhythm into a feeling of cosmic scale. Megre’s *Co-Creation* passages about different energies making something together inform the final exchange.

## 04 The First Quiet Bar

**Logline.** On her final take in a borrowed hall, a young singer hears a real bird trapped above the stage. At the point where her chorus should take off, she stops the playback and gets the room to help it leave.

- `BT-013` · 0:00–0:40. Sara prepares a video take. She starts the piano and the recorded birds in her backing track. A volunteer at the door holds up one finger. They have time for one more.
- `BT-014` · 0:40–1:35. An uneven flutter keeps intruding between phrases. Sara tries to sing through it. In the pause before the chorus she catches the shadow of a wing at a high closed window. She looks from the window to the strip of daylight at the far door.
- `BT-015` · 1:35–2:45. Sara stops the track. She sets the microphone down, and the crew follows her in clearing the doorway and stepping back. The hall stays still long enough for the bird to find the opening.
- `BT-016` · 2:45–3:40. As the bird leaves, the volunteer holds the door open and taps out the beat against the frame. Sara picks up the chorus without the playback. The others enter one by one. End on the open doorway after the song falls quiet.

**Image and sound.** The recorded bird returns in a regular pattern. The live bird keeps breaking it. Give the silence a few seconds to register before the chorus comes back. The EDM version, “The good will prevail EDM,” could move the drop to the moment of release in an alternate cut.

**Source connection.** “Let Goodness Be (Viral Earworm Version)” supplies spring, a dream carried by a song, and birds singing freely. Anastasia’s attention to everyday living things gives Sara a reason to interrupt her performance. The song’s communal ending follows from the crew helping her.

## 05 A Cup With No Name

**Logline.** On her last morning running a neighborhood tea room, a caretaker who has started mixing up names tries to hand over the key and slip out. A regular sets a place for her using the cup she has already packed.

- `BT-017` · 0:00–0:50. Before dawn, Roza sets the tables from habit. She always leaves one place open. At the counter she takes a quick sip of tea with a spoonful of apricot jam. In the back room, her chipped blue cup and the key sit in a cardboard box beside her coat.
- `BT-018` · 0:50–2:25. A regular named Kamal comes in. Roza calls him by another name, then covers by busying herself with the kettle. She puts the key beside the new caretaker’s notebook and reaches for her coat before the others arrive.
- `BT-019` · 2:25–4:20. Kamal takes the blue cup from the open box. He serves her tea with a spoonful of apricot jam on the side, the way she takes it before the room opens. He places it at the empty setting. Roza keeps her coat on for a moment, then sits.
- `BT-020` · 4:20–5:30. Dawn reaches the table. From her chair, Roza slides an extra saucer toward the next empty place. The new caretaker opens the door. Roza stays where she is and smiles when the next visitor steps inside.

**Image and sound.** Open on the familiar order of the room and the sound of a spoon against a saucer. Leave room to hear the cup arrive before bringing in “God You remember me.” Roza’s quiet sense of being held can be read as faith, human care, or both. “About GOD EDM” could support a more communal version in which several regulars each bring something to the table.

**Source connection.** “God You remember me” supplies the fear of losing hold of oneself and the comfort of being remembered. The tea room makes tangible Anastasia’s image of the divine as present in ordinary people and Megre’s image of dawn reaching everyone.

# CapCut editing and delivery

**Documentation checked 2026-11-06.** Use the control names in your installed release. CapCut's guides cover these platform differences. The workflow describes editorial operations that can also be done with separate tracks and manual trims.

| Task | Desktop | Web | Mobile |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Assembly | Import media and use the timeline to split, trim, and reorder. | The browser editor supports a timeline and clip editing. | Use a local project and timeline. Check split and track controls in the installed app. |
| Independent audio | Select a clip and separate or extract audio to a track. | CapCut's guide shows right-click **Separate audio** in the timeline. | Select the clip and use the available **Extract audio** control. |
| Grade and match | The provider documents Adjust controls and a reference-frame Color match option in desktop builds. | Use the exposed adjustments and a reference still for manual comparison. | Use the exposed clip adjustments and compare to the same reference. |
| Titles and captions | Add titles and review captions in the edit. | Auto Captions is documented. Options vary. | Auto Captions is documented. Check timing and safe area on the device. |
| High-resolution export | Desktop has the broadest documented controls. Check source, hardware and codec. | Browser, settings, account and hardware affect available options. | Device, app and source affect available resolutions and frame rates. |

## Cut the short

1. **Set the delivery target.** Record aspect, dimensions, target frame rate, expected codec or container, caption format and audio spec. Inspect source clips and select consistent settings. Preserve originals and keep shot and take IDs.
2. **Build the rough cut.** Import footage and organize by scene and shot. Choose source in/out ranges from usable footage, trim extra generation handles, and assemble by dramatic beats. Verify screen direction, action phase and the intended emotional handoff.
3. **Shape pacing.** Compare holds, alternate cut points, motivated inserts and ellipses. Split clips at the playhead or adjust clip edges. Check orientation and rhythm by playing the cut at speed.
4. **Separate and layer audio.** Extract or mute clip sound where necessary. Add dialogue, room tone or ambience, Foley and effects, and music on editable tracks. Trim audio independently, check sync, and balance essential words against the mix.
5. **Create split edits.** For a J-cut, move or extend incoming audio to begin before the picture cut. For an L-cut, let outgoing audio continue after the incoming picture. Use waveforms, audio handles and fades where appropriate. Review for doubled speech and lost ambience.
6. **Match before grading.** Choose a reference frame and compare the same costume, skin or set areas across cuts. Use available exposure, temperature, tint and contrast controls. Review any automatic match before applying a shared look. Confirm color interpretation when working with HDR or mixed sources.
7. **Add titles and verify captions.** Check names, language, timing, text contrast and safe placement in the delivery crop. Check the actual platform's options for exported captions.
8. **Export and play the file.** Check the exact format, rate, dimensions, duration and audio in the export dialog. Inspect the rendered file outside the editor and compare it to the timeline. Test a representative short export when settings are conditional. Record the final version, source choices and quality findings.

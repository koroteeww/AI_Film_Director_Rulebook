# AI generation failure modes

This table lists plausible contributors and practical checks. The underlying cause of an individual output may be unknown. VBench separates visual consistency from motion and other evaluation dimensions. The provider guide explains reference binding and scoped editing, and SyncNet gives an optional way to inspect lag. This rulebook has not measured the success rate of any repair. [VBench](../sources/bibliography.md#src-vbench) · [EvalVerse](../sources/bibliography.md#src-evalverse) · [Seedance prompt guide](../sources/bibliography.md#src-volc-p25) · [SyncNet](../sources/bibliography.md#src-syncnet)

| ID and symptom | Likely contributors | Prevention in planning | Reference strategy | Prompt mitigation | Detection | Repair or regeneration |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| F01 · Identity drift | Small or occluded face, mixed identity cues or long transformations | Select a stable identity anchor, wardrobe and visible angle. Limit drastic changes in one clip. | Bind one clear identity reference per subject first. Keep other angles in the bible and add only those the mode benefits from. | Use distinct labels, explicit roles and one dominant identity cue per subject. | Compare hairline, facial features and silhouette at start, contact, mid and end, then at adjacent cuts. | Trim to a stable interval, cover with a reaction, or regenerate with one reference and simpler movement. |
| F02 · Costume inconsistency | Competing design descriptions, occlusion or weak garment references | Freeze a costume version and authorize wear, layering or weather changes by beat. | Use a clear medium or full-body reference with visible closure, silhouette and accessories. | Specify stable garment attributes once, then state only the intended changes. | Compare collar, layers, closure, material, color and accessories across all views. | Correct a bounded color problem in post. Regenerate for silhouette or accessory changes that affect continuity. |
| F03 · Disappearing or duplicated objects | Multiple small props, hidden contact, weak ownership or competing emphasis | Give story props IDs, holder, hand, location and explicit state transitions. | Include the prop in the starting composition or add a clear detail reference where the mode permits. | Specify grip and contact sequence, intended occlusion, and terminal ownership. | Scrub around handoff, occlusion and every change in the object's state. | Use a readable insert or cut on compatible phases. Regenerate visible contact that carries the beat. |
| F04 · Spatial discontinuity | Independently invented geometry, new sides of a set or incompatible views | Draw the floor plan, anchors, visibility and routes before generating. | Use approved views or a simple geometry or layout reference supported by the mode. | Bind location geometry and camera viewpoint separately. State what should remain fixed. | Trace anchor positions, doors, depth order, occluders and character routes across the cut. | Re-establish geography or regenerate from the same stage map and reference crop. |
| F05 · Broken screen direction | Mirrored references, an unplanned camera-side change or swapped gaze | Define the dominant action axis, camera side, entry and exit directions, and intentional resets. | Use orientation-checked references with asymmetrical anchors. Check before flipping a still. | State world orientation, screenward travel and gaze target separately. | Watch adjacent shots at speed and compare actor positions, gaze and prop hand around the join. | Use a visible axis reset, neutral or establishing angle. Mirror only after checking text, hand, lighting and all asymmetries. |
| F06 · Lighting inconsistency | Camera-relative light descriptions, undocumented changes or per-clip exposure differences | Map source IDs, world directions, practical states and reference values for the scene. | Use a hero still for each authorized lighting state and known color interpretation. | Describe source position and affected surfaces. Name the only intended state change. | Compare shadow and highlight direction, exposure and subject/background separation across angles. | Match tone or color for stable geometry. Regenerate contradictions in source direction or missing shadows. |
| F07 · Impossible motion or contact | Conflicting paths, too many simultaneous events or endpoints without a feasible transition | Block the action at realistic scale with one primary contact and a clear recovery point. | Use a plausible motion reference, simple blocking video or endpoint pair where supported. | Specify event order, weight shift, contact, object reaction and end state. Simplify secondary motion. | Scrub foot placement, momentum, intersections, grip and state before and after contact. | Split the action at a coherent phase, cover with an insert, or regenerate with smaller motion and less occlusion. |
| F08 · Facial or hand artifacts | Fine detail under rapid motion, partial occlusion or severe crop | Plan moderate movement, readable lighting and enough resolution for the intended screen scale. | Use clean, permitted face and hand references with compatible angles and lighting. | Avoid several overlapping micro-actions. Specify one expressive cue and protect identity. | Review at delivery size and scrub the event. Compare anatomy, features and gaze to the approved reference. | Keep a clean trim or use an alternate motivated framing. Regenerate when an artifact remains in a story-critical detail. |
| F09 · Lip-sync failure | Constant audio offset, wrong speaker or line, mouth motion that differs from the phonemes | Plan short lines, one identifiable speaker and listener coverage. Select a timing reference and language. | Use a stable face reference and permitted clean voice reference if the verified mode supports it. | Specify speaker, line, language and order. Keep camera movement and occlusion simple during speech. | Check visible consonant closures, starts, stops and reactions. Compare to waveform or optional audiovisual sync measurement. | Correct a consistent offset. Use replacement audio with reaction coverage or regenerate when articulation differs. |
| F10 · Audio discontinuity | Independently generated room tone, music, voices, level or perspective | Spot scene audio, choose a continuous bed and keep mix layers editable. | Keep a scene ambience reference and selected voice takes separate from footage. | Request only necessary native audio and specify audible spatial changes. Plan replacement when native tracks are embedded. | Listen without picture across joins. Check voice, ambience, perspective, clipping and tails. | Mute or replace incompatible native tracks, crossfade ambience, and align cues or bridge the cut. |
| F11 · Incompatible start and end frames | Unrelated lens, geometry, identity, light or impossible pose changes between frames | Plan a plausible route and camera motion before locking a second frame. | Generate the end as a controlled variation from the same approved visual state when possible. | Name the interpolated action and endpoint state. Use dedicated frame roles only when the pair is compatible. | Compare both frames for world, orientation and timing, then inspect interpolation for morphs, cuts or skipped action. | Use first frame plus soft references, revise the pair, or use two shots with an editorial transition. |
| F12 · Unmotivated or uncontrolled camera movement | Ambiguous language, a conflicting motion reference, or excessive choreography | Name the dramatic reason, move type, subject path, start view and terminal frame. | Supply one camera reference only if its camera behavior is useful and roles can be bound. | Specify locked-off, rotation, translation or zoom and distinguish from subject motion. | Check horizon, parallax, movement start, destination, focus and attention in the edit. | Use the stable part of the take, reframe if the source permits, or regenerate a simpler move or locked view. |
| F13 · Contradictory prompt or reference | Prompt roles, endpoints, styles or motion descriptions require incompatible outcomes | Check hard scene constraints against prompt order, camera path and each reference role. | Drop conflicting or redundant references and give retained references distinct roles. | Resolve each contradiction before generation. Keep one main action and one camera instruction unless coordinated. | Review the brief before submitting and compare rejected outcomes to the exact conflicting requirements. | Keep the higher-priority story or continuity requirement. Change one variable, retry on a short test, then compare. |

## Triage order

1. Review the shot at delivery size and in the cut. Record the failure ID, evidence timecode, severity, planned state and observed state.
2. Protect story-critical information, intended performance and continuity before improving a cosmetic issue. Accept a flaw only if it is outside the usable trim or the review establishes that it does not interfere.
3. Try trim or coverage when the beat and continuity survive. Use a bounded edit for a local issue and compare the entire output. Regenerate if identity, geography, performance or physical contact cannot be restored.
4. Change one likely contributor first, keep the previous settings and reference IDs, and compare the next take. If the same failure repeats, simplify the coverage, change modes, or split the action at a motivated boundary.
